The Basics

A resume has one job: to earn you an interview.

Hiring managers often review many applications for a single role and may spend only seconds on each one. Make yours easy to scan, easy to trust and clearly relevant.

Clear

One to two pages, a clean layout, consistent fonts and clear headings so the key points are seen first.

Relevant

Tailored to each job. Use the same keywords as the advert and lead with the experience that matches it.

Honest and proven

Show results, with numbers where you can, and never include anything you cannot back up in an interview.

What to Include

The sections every resume needs.

  1. Contact details: your name, phone number, professional email address and city.
  2. Professional summary: two or three lines on who you are, what you do well and the role you want.
  3. Work experience: most recent first. Describe what you achieved, not only what you were responsible for.
  4. Education and training: qualifications, certificates and relevant courses.
  5. Skills: the skills the advert asks for that you genuinely have.
  6. Referees: two people who know your work, or “available on request”.

No work experience yet? Lead with your education, projects, volunteering, internships and leadership roles. Show what you can do and how quickly you learn.

Do & Don’t

Small choices that make a big difference.

Do

  • Tailor your resume to every job you apply for.
  • Start each bullet with a strong action verb.
  • Use numbers: people served, time saved, targets met.
  • Use a professional email address, such as your name.
  • Save and send it as a PDF with a clear file name, like Ama-Mensah-Resume.pdf.
  • Ask someone else to proofread it.

Don’t

  • Use one generic resume for every application.
  • Write long paragraphs or crowd the page with tiny text.
  • Include a photo, age, marital status or ID number unless the job asks for it.
  • Leave unexplained gaps or exaggerate your experience.
  • Make spelling or grammar mistakes.
  • Use fancy fonts, bright colours or heavy graphics.
